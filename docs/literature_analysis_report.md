# Literature Analysis: OpenAlex Metadata Quality and Global Coverage

This report synthesizes the literature search findings conducted for the project **"Analyzing Turkish LIS Metadata Quality in OpenAlex"**. The search focused on identifying methodological models, global coverage biases, and specific metadata quality issues (affiliation, language, document type) relevant to the "Sustainability in Information and Records Management" symposium theme.

---

## 1. Key Methodological Models (Direct Parallels)

These studies provide the "Gold Standard" for our current research, as they apply similar comparison frameworks between OpenAlex and proprietary databases (Scopus/WoS) for specific geographical regions or domains.

| Source | Findings & Relevance |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Alonso-Álvarez & van Eck (2025)**<br>DOI: 10.1162/qss.a.396 | **The "Africa" Project:** Directly compares metadata completeness (affiliations, references, funders) for African research. Found that while OpenAlex has better coverage, metadata accuracy is lower for non-WoS/Scopus indexed items. |
| **Doğan & Sezen (2026)**<br>DOI: 10.47989/ir31iconf64207 | **The "Turkish" Context:** A local study by Turkish authors focusing on publication type (43% discrepancy) and language (3.3% mismatch) errors in OpenAlex. |
| **Madi et al. (2026)**<br>DOI: 10.60144/v7i.2026.173 | **The "Brazil" Proxy:** Evaluates OpenAlex as an alternative for evaluating Brazilian science. Highlights the inclusion of local languages (Portuguese) but warns about automatic indexing errors. |

## 2. Identified Metadata Quality Issues

The literature confirms several recurring gaps in OpenAlex metadata that align with our initial observations of Turkish LIS journals.

### A. Affiliation & Institutional Mapping
- **Najko Jahn (2025):** Reports a significant decreasing trend in affiliation metadata coverage following the "Walden" update (Nov 2025). 
- **Impact:** This explains why we find many Turkish records with missing or incorrectly mapped university affiliations.

### B. Linguistic & Geographic Biases
- **Céspedes et al. (2025):** JASIST study showing OpenAlex overestimates English by wrongly labeling non-English works as English (3.3% error rate in some samples).
- **Maddi et al. (2024):** OpenAlex indexes 34,217 OA journals (vs 7k in Scopus), providing much better inclusivity but still showing systematic underrepresentation of the Global South.

### C. Document Type Inconsistencies
- **Rizzetto & Peroni (2023):** Discovered high inconsistency rates between OpenAlex and OpenCitations Meta during integration. 
- **Scheidsteger & Haunschild (2023):** Noted that 90% of MAG documents were transferred correctly, but reclassifications to "journal-article" often require manual verification.

## 3. Sustainability and "Tradition to Future"

To align with the symposium's theme, the following connections have been identified:

- **Open Infrastructure Sustainability:** Ruttenberg (2021) argues that the sustainability of open scholarly infrastructures (like OpenAlex) requires its own funding and metadata ecosystem.
- **National Visibility:** Building a sustainable "Future" for Turkish LIS research depends on the "Tradition" of accurate indexing. If open databases like OpenAlex contain poor metadata for national journals, our historical (traditional) scientific output becomes invisible to global future-oriented AI and bibliometric tools.

---

## 4. Recommendations for Literature Gap

1. **The Comparative Gap:** While Africa and Brazil have been studied, a comprehensive, multi-journal quantitative quality audit for **Turkish LIS journals** (specifically comparing "Bilgi Dünyası", "Türk Kütüphaneciliği", etc.) in OpenAlex is not yet prominently featured in the international literature.
2. **Specific Error Patterns:** Most international studies focus on *completeness*. Our opportunity is to focus on *accuracy levels* for Turkish-specific characters and institutional names.
3. **Open Access Infrastructure:** Position the paper as a "stress test" for the sustainability of open bibliometric infrastructures in non-Western contexts.
